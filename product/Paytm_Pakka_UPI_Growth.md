# Paytm Pakka: Building Primary-App Preference for UPI

## At a glance

**The challenge:** Many users who have used Paytm open another UPI app
first out of habit. Paytm's offers may be visible only after opening the
app, while merchant uncertainty about deductions, support and payment
status can undermine trust.

**The proposal:** Paytm Pakka combines predictable customer cashback
with merchant transparency, reliability and benefits, giving both sides
a reason to choose Paytm first.

**Team:** Team Paytm Karo\
**Source:** Paytm UPI Growth Challenge, Round 1, Track A --- Build
Primary-App Preference.

## Research and problem discovery

The team collected **50 voice-of-customer inputs**, mainly in Tamil
Nadu: 29 consumers and 21 merchants. The core consumer cohort comprised
15 people who had used Paytm but mostly paid through another app.

In this cohort: - 0 of 15 cited a Paytm failure or dislike. - 12 of 15
opened Paytm only when an offer or cashback triggered it. - The main
competing app often won through a known reason before the QR scan:
reward rules, ecosystem, habit or AutoPay. - 14 of 21 merchants said
their core need was knowing whether money had actually arrived. - 3 of
21 merchants had left Paytm over fixable issues such as technical
faults, Soundbox or support problems, or unclear deductions.

These are directional findings from the team's small VOC sample, not
market-wide estimates.

## Core insight

The choice is often made at the counter, before any payment app is
opened. Paytm's reason to choose it can be hidden inside the app and
change by campaign. Meanwhile, merchant uncertainty about received
amounts, deductions and settlement can affect which QR is displayed
prominently.

**Core problem:** Secondary users have no fixed, visible reason to
choose Paytm at the counter, while merchant trust can be weakened by
unclear payment and fee information.

## Proposed solution: Paytm Pakka

Paytm Pakka offers "Pakka cashback" for customers and "Pakka
transparency, reliability and benefits" for merchants.

### Customer experience

-   **Fixed cashback tiers:** Proposed rewards of ₹10 for payments above
    ₹200, ₹15 above ₹500 and ₹30 above ₹1,000, using a predictable rule
    rather than "up to" campaign offers.
-   **Scan-first app:** Open directly to a full-screen scanner with a
    prominent Scan & Pay action.
-   **Visible rewards at checkout:** Use QR standees, Soundbox stickers
    and online checkout badges so the benefit is visible before the user
    chooses an app.
-   **Pakka Meter:** Show how much the user has earned over time.
-   **Pakka Goal:** Let users assign cashback to a goal such as a
    recharge or electricity bill.
-   **Pakka Week:** Reward Paytm payments on five days in a week.
-   **Pakka Status:** Explain payment success, failure or pending status
    clearly.

The cashback tiers, caps and mechanics are team proposals to test, not
existing Paytm offers.

### Merchant experience

-   **Paytm FeeBack:** Offer fee credits or benefits as Paytm GMV grows,
    with extra credit when Paytm becomes the primary QR.
-   **Paytm ClearMoney:** Show daily received amounts, deductions,
    pending amounts and settled amounts, with an explanation for each
    deduction.
-   **Paytm Guarantee:** Show a verifiable payment status and explain
    what to do when a payment is pending, reducing the risk of a
    customer being asked to pay twice.
-   **Paytm Merchant Assist:** Provide named local support and clear
    follow-up for device or payment issues.

## Two-sided growth loop

1.  Merchants trust Paytm's payment status and fee transparency.
2.  More merchants display Paytm's QR prominently.
3.  Customers see predictable cashback before scanning.
4.  More customers choose Paytm and complete payments.
5.  Merchants earn FeeBack as eligible Paytm GMV grows.

The proposal is designed to create durable preference through habit,
checkout visibility and merchant trust, rather than relying only on
temporary cashback.

## Target segments

**Consumers:** People who used Paytm UPI in the past 90 days but
habitually open another app, especially for frequent, low- or
medium-value payments such as tea, food, autos and kirana purchases.

**Merchants:** Small, busy counters that run Paytm alongside other QR
codes and need confidence in payment confirmation, deductions,
settlement and support.

Reward optimisers and AutoPay-heavy users are treated as later or
non-primary segments in the submitted proposal.

## Pilot and measurement

The proposal recommends a one-city pilot over **eight weeks, followed by
four weeks of reduced cashback**.

-   Compare customer control, cashback tiers alone, and the full Pakka
    package.
-   Include 200 merchant counters using FeeBack, ClearMoney, Guarantee
    and Assist, with matched controls.
-   Measure Paytm payments and GMV per user, merchants with Paytm as the
    front QR, merchant churn and support tickets.
-   Reduce cashback in weeks 9--12. If at least half the increase
    persists, that would suggest preference rather than purely temporary
    reward response.

The persistence threshold is a proposed test criterion, not a measured
result.

## Economics and guardrails

The proposal suggests capping and targeting cashback, for example with a
monthly cap per user, and keeping cashback inside Paytm balance.
Merchant benefits should be tied to incremental Paytm GMV. The scanner,
counter visibility and merchant trust are intended to remain useful even
if cashback is reduced.

The executive summary includes an illustrative estimate of potential
payment and GMV upside. It explicitly notes that user counts, payment
frequency and ticket mix must be validated using Paytm data; these
estimates should not be presented as realised impact.

## Key product decisions

-   Solve the decision before the scan, not only after users enter the
    app.
-   Make rewards predictable and easy to remember.
-   Treat merchant trust as part of consumer growth, not a separate
    operations problem.
-   Combine cashback with durable assets: scanner accessibility,
    checkout visibility and transparent settlement.
-   Test whether behaviour persists after incentives are reduced.

## Limitations and assumptions

This is a team case-study proposal, not a launched Paytm feature.
Research is based on a small directional VOC sample. Reward slabs, fee
credits, caps and projected business impact are hypotheses to validate
in a controlled pilot.

No Figma URL was provided in the submitted executive summary, so no
design link is included.

## Interview talking points

-   Why does Paytm need primary-app preference rather than another
    cashback campaign?
-   How do merchant trust and customer behaviour reinforce each other?
-   How would you distinguish long-term preference from incentive-driven
    usage?
-   What would you measure during the cashback taper?
-   How would you design a fair matched-control experiment across users
    and merchants?
-   How would you manage the trade-off between cashback cost and
    incremental GMV?
